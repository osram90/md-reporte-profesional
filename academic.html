Molecular Dynamics Simulations of RSV-F Protein-Ligand Complexes: A 10 ns Study of Structural Stability and Ligand Compatibility

Computational analysis of ten ligand candidates using GROMACS 2025.3 with GPU acceleration

Computational Chemistry Laboratory

October 10, 2026

Molecular Systems Investigation

Abstract

Molecular dynamics simulations were performed on ten protein-ligand complexes of the respiratory syncytial virus fusion protein (RSV-F) to assess structural stability and binding compatibility. Each system was simulated for 10 nanoseconds using GROMACS 2025.3 with the AMBER99sb-ildn force field and TIP3P water model, with GPU acceleration enabling efficient computational throughput. Root mean square deviation (RMSD) analysis demonstrated structural stability in all systems, with final values ranging from 1.69 to 1.88 Å, well below the 2.0 Å threshold for biological relevance. Root mean square fluctuation (RMSF) analysis identified rigidified binding sites with average fluctuations between 0.38 and 0.51 Å. Temperature was maintained at 310 K (±0.3 K) throughout simulations, confirming robust thermostat control. Vibegron control, chlorhexidine, and bictegravir demonstrated superior stability metrics and warrant priority for experimental validation. This computational study provides a systematic basis for rational drug design targeting RSV-F.

Introduction

Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) represents a significant public health burden, particularly in pediatric populations, causing severe respiratory tract infections that result in substantial morbidity and mortality globally. The viral fusion protein (F) constitutes the primary target for entry inhibition and has emerged as an attractive therapeutic target for antiviral development. Structure-based virtual screening combined with molecular dynamics simulations provides a computational framework for identifying promising ligand candidates prior to resource-intensive experimental validation.

Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations enable the investigation of conformational dynamics, stability assessment, and protein-ligand interaction mechanisms at atomic resolution. The present study applied MD simulations to evaluate ten structurally diverse ligand candidates against RSV-F, assessing their compatibility and long-term stability within the protein binding environment.

Materials and Methods

Simulation Setup

Molecular dynamics simulations were conducted using GROMACS 2025.3 (Berendsen et al., 1995) with the AMBER99sb-ildn force field (Lindorff-Larsen et al., 2010) and TIP3P water model (Jorgensen et al., 1983). All simulations were performed at 310 K (body temperature) and 1 atm pressure, representing physiological conditions.

System Preparation

Protein-ligand complexes were prepared using standard preprocessing protocols:

  1. Topology generation via gmx pdb2gmx (AMBER99sb-ildn)
  2. Cubic simulation box definition with 1.2 nm minimum distance
  3. Solvation with TIP3P water molecules
  4. Ionic neutralization with Na+ and Cl− (physiological ionic strength)

Equilibration Protocol

Minimization: 5,000 steps steepest descent (EM)
NVT: 50,000 steps at 310 K (v-rescale thermostat)
NPT: 50,000 steps at 1 atm (Berendsen barostat)
Production: 5,000,000 steps (10 ns total)

Analysis Methods

Root Mean Square Deviation (RMSD)

RMSD was computed for the protein backbone Cα atoms relative to the initial structure:

RMSD(t) = √[1/N Σ(r_i(t) - r_i^ref)²]

where r_i(t) is the position of atom i at time t, r_i^ref is the reference position, and N is the number of atoms. RMSD convergence to equilibrium values <2.0 Å indicates structural stability (Dror et al., 2009).

Root Mean Square Fluctuation (RMSF)

Per-residue flexibility was assessed via RMSF:

RMSF_i = √[1/T Σ(r_i(t) - <r_i>)²]

where T is the simulation length and <r_i> is the time-averaged position. RMSF identifies dynamic regions and binding site rigidity.

Binding Site Rigidity

RMSF analysis revealed that protein regions occupied by bound ligands exhibited lower fluctuation values compared to solvent-exposed regions, indicating ligand-induced stabilization of the binding site. Average RMSF values ranged from 0.38 Å (Vibegron_Ctrl) to 0.51 Å (Vibegron), consistent with maintained structural organization of the active site.

Figure 2. Average RMSF values per residue for all systems. Lower RMSF in binding site regions (Å) indicates reduced conformational flexibility induced by ligand binding, essential for maintaining specificity and binding affinity.

Thermodynamic Control

Temperature was maintained at 310 K (±0.3 K) throughout all simulations, demonstrating precise thermostat coupling and reproducible conditions. Energy analysis confirmed convergence without systematic drift, indicating thermodynamic equilibrium had been reached.

Figure 3. Average system temperature for each simulation. Target temperature of 310 K (green line) was maintained with minimal deviation, confirming effective thermostat control and reproducible conditions across all ten systems.

Discussion

The present computational study demonstrates that all ten evaluated ligand candidates maintain stable protein-ligand complexes throughout 10 ns molecular dynamics simulations at physiological temperature and pressure. This finding has significant implications for identifying viable drug candidates targeting RSV-F.

Structural Stability Implications

RMSD convergence below 2.0 Å in all cases indicates that ligand binding does not induce pathological conformational changes in the protein. The rapid convergence observed (2–3 ns) suggests effective equilibration and minimization of system artifacts. This metric is particularly important as RMSD values exceeding 3.0 Å would suggest dissociation or major rearrangement events incompatible with sustained protein-ligand interactions.

Ranking of Candidates

Three systems demonstrated superior stability metrics warranting priority for experimental validation:

  1. Vibegron control (RMSD 1.69 Å, RMSF 0.38 Å): Established reference with excellent stability, validating computational methodology
  2. Chlorhexidine (RMSD 1.75 Å, RMSF 0.42 Å): Natural compound demonstrating surprising compatibility with RSV-F binding site
  3. Bictegravir (RMSD 1.77 Å, RMSF 0.43 Å): Antirretroviral agent showing unexpected protein-ligand stability

Conclusions

This molecular dynamics study of ten protein-ligand complexes targeting RSV-F demonstrates:

  • Universal structural stability across all evaluated ligands (RMSD <2.0 Å)
  • Binding site rigidification consistent with productive ligand interactions
  • Thermodynamic stability under physiological conditions
  • Identification of three high-priority candidates for experimental validation

These computational findings provide a rational basis for prioritizing resources toward experimental validation of the most promising candidates. Future studies should incorporate free energy calculations and extended simulations (>50 ns) to more thoroughly sample the conformational landscape.
